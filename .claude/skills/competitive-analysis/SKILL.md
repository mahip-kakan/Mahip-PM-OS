# Skill: Competitive Analysis

## Trigger
Activate when the user says "analyze competitor," "competitive analysis for," "how does [company] approach [problem]," or "what are competitors doing with [feature/space]."

---

## Behavior

### Step 1 — Clarify scope
Ask:
1. Which competitor(s) are we analyzing? (Or: who are the top 3 in this space?)
2. What specifically are we trying to learn — pricing, feature approach, positioning, go-to-market?
3. What decision will this analysis inform?
4. Do you have any existing research I should build on (in `Knowledge/competitors/`)?

---

### Step 2 — Structure the analysis

For each competitor, use the Smart / Weak / Implications framework:

#### [Competitor Name]

**Smart moves** — What are they doing well? What has clearly worked?
- [Observation + evidence or reasoning]
- [Observation]

**Weak spots** — Where are they exposed? What are their users complaining about?
- [Weakness + evidence or reasoning]
- [Weakness]

**Strategic implications for us** — Given what they're doing, what should we do differently, faster, or better?
- [Implication]
- [Implication]

---

### Step 3 — Cross-competitor synthesis

After analyzing each competitor:

**Where the market is converging:**
[Features or approaches that every major player now has — these are likely table stakes]

**Where there's differentiation opportunity:**
[Gaps nobody is filling well, or underserved user segments]

**Who is the benchmark we should worry most about:**
[One competitor + reason — usually the one with the best product, not just market share]

---

### Step 4 — Recommendation

Given the competitive landscape, what should we do?
- **Invest in:** [Areas where we can win]
- **Match (not differentiate):** [Table stakes we can't ignore]
- **Avoid:** [Where the market is too crowded or we have no right to win]

---

## Important Caveats

- Always flag when an observation is inferred (from public sources, app reviews, job postings) vs. directly confirmed.
- Never present unconfirmed claims about competitors as facts. Use "reportedly," "appears to," "based on public information."
- Competitor research decays fast. Date every entry in `Knowledge/competitors/`.

---

## Save to Knowledge

After completing the analysis, save a summary to:
`Knowledge/competitors/[competitor-name].md`

Include the date so future sessions know when this was researched.
